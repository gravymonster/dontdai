## Clinical and service benchmarks

- **E1 — NCI, Finding Cancer Care / Getting a Second Opinion.** Second opinions may confirm or change a plan, increase information and confidence, and work best when the current physician is involved in providing records. https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/managing-care/finding-cancer-care
- **E2 — Lipitz-Snyderman et al., 2023, Cancer Medicine.** Retrospective review of 120 oncology cases: 42 had clinically meaningful treatment changes with expected positive outcomes; diagnostic changes leading to treatment change occurred in 13. The study was single-center and retrospective. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10134380/
- **E3 — Dana-Farber / AccessHope Online Second Opinion FAQ.** Coordinator-led record collection; review begins after complete records; written opinion delivered to the patient and identified local provider; record collection can take up to 14 days and expert review generally takes another 7–14 business days. https://www.dana-farber.org/appointments-second-opinions/second-opinion-program/faq
- **E4 — Cleveland Clinic Virtual Second Opinions.** Secure account, nurse intake, consent-based record collection, specialist matching, written report, and optional physician discussion; typically about two weeks after intake. https://my.clevelandclinic.org/online-services/virtual-second-opinions
- **E5 — Memorial Sloan Kettering sample remote second-opinion report.** Shows a shared patient/provider report with scope limitations, expert identity, medical history, patient questions, recommendations, and a clear statement that the treating physician directs care. https://www.mskcc.org/sites/default/files/node/187201/documents/msk-remote-second-opinion_sample-report.pdf
- **E6 — MD Anderson new-patient intake.** Records categories include pathology reports, imaging reports, clinic/progress/H&P notes, treatment records, laboratories, molecular testing/biomarkers, additional testing, identity and insurance. https://my.mdanderson.org/RequestAppointment?micro=mch_hp_selfref_foot
- **E7 — Mayo Clinic, Tips for Seeking a Second Opinion.** Advises assembling scans, notes, reports, treatment history, blood tests and pathology, defining the purpose of the opinion, and sharing conclusions with the current doctor. https://connect.mayoclinic.org/blog/cancer-education-center/newsfeed-post/tips-for-seeking-a-second-opinion/
- **E8 — Chan et al., 2023, patient navigation umbrella review.** Navigation improves screening participation and time to diagnosis/treatment; emerging evidence supports quality of life and satisfaction benefits in later phases. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37358040/

## Health literacy and communication

- **E9 — AHRQ Health Literacy Universal Precautions Toolkit, third edition.** Recommends making information and services understandable and usable for everyone, simplifying communication, confirming understanding and supporting navigation. https://www.ahrq.gov/health-literacy/improve/precautions/index.html
- **E10 — AHRQ Teach-Back.** Teach-back checks whether the clinician explained information clearly by asking the patient or caregiver to explain key actions in their own words. https://www.ahrq.gov/patient-safety/reports/engage/interventions/teachback.html
- **E11 — NCI Communication in Cancer Care (health professional version).** Supports plain language, limiting information per interaction, checking preferences, teach-back, professional interpreters, and patient-centered question elicitation. https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/coping/adjusting-to-cancer/communication-hp-pdq
- **E12 — NCI Questions to Ask about Cancer Treatment.** Provides a patient-friendly question framework covering treatment rationale, benefits/risks, timing, trials and side effects. https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/treatment/questions

## Privacy, consent, security and records

- **E13 — HHS, consent vs authorization.** HIPAA consent and authorization are distinct; a valid authorization requires specified elements. https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/264/what-is-the-difference-between-consent-and-authorization/index.html
- **E14 — HHS, individual right of access.** A patient can direct records to a third party; covered entities generally must act within 30 days, although operational delivery may be faster. https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/access/index.html
- **E15 — HHS, authorization versus directed access.** A patient-directed access request generally compels disclosure (subject to exceptions), whereas a third-party HIPAA authorization permits disclosure; the two have different required elements and fee/timeliness rules. https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/2041/why-depend-on-the-individuals-right/index.html
- **E16 — HHS, cloud computing and HIPAA.** A cloud provider handling ePHI is generally a business associate even if it cannot decrypt the data; a BAA and documented risk analysis/management are required. https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/special-topics/health-information-technology/cloud-computing/index.html
- **E17 — HHS, HIPAA Security Rule and risk analysis.** Administrative, physical and technical safeguards must protect ePHI; risk analysis is foundational and ongoing. https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/security/guidance/guidance-risk-analysis/index.html
- **E18 — NIST SP 800-66 Rev. 2.** Maps HIPAA Security Rule requirements to practical cybersecurity activities and NIST controls. https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/66/r2/final
- **E19 — HHS, disclosure for treatment by fax/email/phone.** Treatment disclosures are permitted with reasonable safeguards, but the recipient and sender must be verified and the exact legal relationship remains fact-dependent. https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/482/does-hipaa-permit-a-doctor-to-share-patient-information-for-treatment-over-the-phone/index.html
- **E20 — HHS, PHI disposal.** Paper PHI should be rendered unreadable and unreconstructable (for example, shredding); electronic media need appropriate clearing, purging or destruction. https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/faq/575/what-does-hipaa-require-of-covered-entities-when-they-dispose-information/index.html
- **E21 — HHS, business associate agreement sample provisions.** BAAs should define permitted uses, safeguards, incident reporting, subcontractors, return/destruction and termination. https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/covered-entities/sample-business-associate-agreement-provisions/index.html
- **E22 — HHS, 42 CFR Part 2 final rule fact sheet.** As of February 16, 2026, Part 2 consent and redisclosure requirements apply to covered SUD records and should be included in counsel’s consent review. https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/regulatory-initiatives/fact-sheet-42-cfr-part-2-final-rule/index.html
- **E23 — Google Workspace HIPAA BAA.** Drive and other named services may be covered services only under the executed BAA and the customer’s compliant configuration and use. https://workspace.google.com/terms/2015/1/hipaa_baa/
- **E24 — Google Workspace HIPAA compliance guidance.** Customer must accept the BAA, determine applicability and configure services; specific-recipient sharing is preferred over link sharing. https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/compliance/hipaa-compliance-with-google-workspace-and-cloud-identity
- **E25 — ONC, Information Blocking.** Requests for electronic health information and available manners of fulfillment are governed by current Cures Act regulations; counsel should confirm whether and how the rules apply to each party. https://healthit.gov/information-blocking/

## Digital health, advertising and regulation

- **E26 — FDA, Clinical Decision Support Software Guidance, January 2026.** Non-device CDS must meet all four statutory criteria, including being intended for HCP use and enabling independent review of the basis; software interpreting NGS patterns or clinical implications may not satisfy the first criterion. https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/clinical-decision-support-software
- **E27 — HHS, online tracking technologies.** Tracking technologies on authenticated health pages may access PHI; regulated entities must ensure permitted disclosures and Security Rule safeguards. The guidance reflects a 2024 court ruling on part of the unauthenticated-page analysis. https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/hipaa-online-tracking/index.html
- **E28 — FTC Health Breach Notification Rule, 2024 amendments.** Clarifies application to many health apps and similar technologies not covered by HIPAA and covers unauthorized disclosures as well as security intrusions. https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/health-breach-notification-rule
- **E29 — FTC Health Products Compliance Guidance.** Objective health claims must be truthful, non-misleading and supported before dissemination; health and safety claims generally require strong substantiation. https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance
- **E30 — FTC pixel-tracking enforcement overview.** Highlights enforcement involving health-data sharing for advertising and the risks of third-party tracking pixels. https://www.ftc.gov/policy/advocacy-research/tech-at-ftc/2023/03/lurking-beneath-surface-hidden-impacts-pixel-tracking
- **E31 — HHS Telehealth, licensing across state lines.** State rules vary; providers should verify the patient’s location and consent, and use a full license, registration, compact or applicable exception as allowed. https://telehealth.hhs.gov/licensure/licensing-across-state-lines

## Hereditary cancer referral

- **E32 — NCI Genetic Testing Fact Sheet.** Hereditary cancer testing generally begins with an affected family member when possible; pre-test counseling and written informed consent address medical implications, risks, benefits, alternatives and privacy. https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/genetics/genetic-testing-fact-sheet
- **E33 — FDA Direct-to-Consumer Tests.** DTC cancer-predisposition testing has important limitations and should not be used alone to make treatment decisions; qualified clinician/genetic-counselor discussion is recommended. https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/in-vitro-diagnostics/direct-consumer-tests
